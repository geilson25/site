About

A systemic view of security, shaped by more than engineering.

Geilson Silva is a Master's candidate in Software Engineering at CESAR School and a Research Fellow at the CISSA/CESAR Cybersecurity Competence Center, an Embrapii-affiliated unit dedicated to applied research in cybersecurity. His academic path bridges computer science, behavioral analysis, and organizational security — an interest in how human behavior manifests, and sometimes betrays itself, within digital systems.

Research & Achievements

From hypothesis to proof of concept.

Geilson's master's research investigates the use of Process Mining to detect insider threats, with a specific emphasis on the behavioral precursors of data exfiltration. His work proposes a hybrid detection pipeline that merges event-log-based process discovery with unsupervised machine learning — including Isolation Forest, PCA, and K-Means — to flag anomalous behavioral patterns before data loss occurs.

His paper, "DT-Leak: Process Mining-Based Behavioral Detection of Data Exfiltration," was accepted at SBSEG 2026 (Simpósio Brasileiro de Segurança da Informação e de Sistemas Computacionais). He is also a co-author of "Cyber Threat Intelligence Sharing: Challenges and Opportunities," examining the operational and organizational barriers to effective CTI collaboration.

Interdisciplinary Interests

Security is a human system, not only a technical one.

Beyond his technical work, Geilson maintains a strong interest in symbolism, literature, and epistemology. He approaches cybersecurity not only as an engineering discipline but as a human and systemic one — where technical controls, organizational culture, and individual behavior interact. This humanistic lens informs how he frames research questions and interprets behavioral data.

Current Focus

Completing the experimental validation phase of his POC, integrating Wazuh, Suricata, and ELK Stack telemetry into a unified behavioral dataset. Also contributing to Knowtify, a mobile wellbeing application for healthcare professionals at Hospital Santa Joana.
