Research

Investigating the behavioral fingerprints of insider threats.

Process Mining and machine learning, applied to system event logs, to surface the deviations that precede data exfiltration.

Overview

Why insider threats resist detection.

Insider threats — malicious, negligent, or the product of compromised credentials — remain among the hardest security risks to catch, because the actors involved already hold legitimate access. Signature- and rule-based systems struggle to identify the subtle behavioral shifts that come before an exfiltration event.

This research closes that gap by applying Process Mining to reconstruct the actual behavioral processes of users from system event logs, then comparing them against expected or historical baselines. Deviations in sequence, timing, and flow become the raw signal for anomaly detection.

What is Process Mining, here?

Reading behavior from the log, not the policy.

Process Mining uses event log data to discover, monitor, and improve real processes. In a security context, it reconstructs "as-is" behavioral models of how users interact with systems and data — revealing patterns, and breaks in pattern, that conventional log analysis misses.

Methodology

A hypothetico-deductive approach, in three phases.

  • 01

    Hypothesis formulation

    Defining behavioral indicators expected to precede exfiltration events, grounded in prior literature and threat-modeling frameworks.

  • 02

    POC development

    Building a pipeline that ingests security telemetry, applies process discovery, and runs anomaly detection models.

  • 03

    Experimental testing

    Validating detection accuracy against simulated and historical insider-threat scenarios — measuring precision, recall, and false-positive rate.
